DESCRIPTION
In August 1988, a man without papers sat down in Terminal 1 of Paris–Charles de Gaulle Airport. Mehran Karimi Nasseri would live there for almost eighteen years. His story loosely inspired Steven Spielberg's The Terminal (2004), but the real one is quieter, sadder and far less certain.

This CAKIR documentary follows what is verified, what is disputed, and what may never be known: the missing refugee papers, the legal limbo, the red bench in Terminal 1, the name "Sir Alfred", the Hollywood deal, the papers he refused to sign, and his return to the airport where he died on 12 November 2022.

A note on accuracy: Nasseri was not locked in a sealed transit zone. He lived in a public area of Terminal 1. Parts of his own account of his early life were disputed by later reporting, and the film marks them as such. Only the two photographs of Nasseri (2004, 2005) and the period photographs of Terminal 1 are historical. All moving footage is modern and is captioned on screen as illustrative or with its date and location.
